demos

Brownian motion & the diffusion coefficient

The same physics my DDM pipeline pulls out of correlation functions, made interactive: a cloud of particles executes a random walk, and the diffusion coefficient D sets how fast that cloud spreads. Adjust particle size and temperature — related to D by the Stokes–Einstein equation, D = kBT / (6πηr) — and watch the motion and the mean-squared-displacement (MSD) plot respond together.

D = µm²/s  (in water, Stokes–Einstein)

Red: measured mean-squared displacement across the particle ensemble. Dashed grey: the theoretical prediction MSD = 4Dt for 2D diffusion, whose slope is exactly D. Simulated time is sped up for visibility, and the run resets once the cloud fills the frame.

Optical-trapping & DDM analysis pipeline

A structured xarray Dataset holding optical trapping and DDM data across trap count, laser power, and trial dimensions. Includes a "closest pair" outlier-detection method built for small sample sizes, interactive domain-by-domain review tooling for flagging and NaN-ing bad points, and correct error propagation through the nonlinear 1/τ transform.
